Case study · Mobile app

Urban Access Map App

A digital tool that highlights accessible walking routes, so that everyone, from wheelchair users to parents with strollers, can navigate safely.

RoleUI/UX design
PlatformMobile
FocusAccessible walking routes
ToolsFigma, FigJam

The brief

01

The problem

  • Sidewalks are narrow, uneven or blocked; many lack ramps and crossings feel unsafe.
  • Existing maps and navigation apps don't account for these limits.
  • How can a platform map walking routes and also evaluate their accessibility for different groups?
02

Design goals

  • Highlight accessible and safe walking routes across neighbourhoods.
  • Filters for different needs: wheelchair, elderly, stroller.
  • Clear visual indicators.
  • Real-time community feedback, with citizens flagging unsafe paths.
03

Impact

  • Citizens: safer and more inclusive mobility.
  • Planners: actionable insights to prioritise upgrades, such as adding ramps or fixing pavements.
  • The city: equity, by letting all residents move independently.

Target users

Wheelchair users and persons with disabilities

Need routes free of barriers like stairs and steep slopes.

Elderly residents

Require smoother sidewalks and safe crossings.

Parents with strollers

Prefer wider paths and pedestrian-friendly areas.

City planners

Analyse accessibility data to improve infrastructure.

Research and personas

I studied accessibility-focused projects such as Wheelmap and AccessMap Seattle to learn their best practices. Local observations in Addis Ababa revealed uneven pavements, a lack of ramps and poor pedestrian crossings.

Iconography

Outline icons with an orange accent. The active tab sits in a soft orange circle, and map controls are white circular buttons that stay readable over the map.

UX principles

Usability principles behind the screens, and where they show up.

01

Flexibility

Routes can be filtered for different needs: wheelchair, elderly, stroller.

02

Visibility

Color-coded route layers on the map show which paths suit which need, without reading a legend first.

03

Match with the real world

Selecting accessibility needs comes first in the flow, then suggested routes and turn-by-turn guidance.

04

Community feedback

People can flag unsafe paths, so the map improves with use.

Reflection and what's next

Future improvements

  • AI-based detection of sidewalk conditions from street-level imagery.
  • Crowdsourced route validation with gamification incentives.
  • Integration with public transport apps for full door-to-door accessibility.
