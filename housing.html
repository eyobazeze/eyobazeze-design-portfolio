Case study · Mobile app

Affordable Housing & Income Map App

An interactive map that compares household income levels with housing costs across neighbourhoods, helping citizens, policymakers and planners make informed decisions.

RoleUI/UX design
PlatformMobile
FocusRent vs. income
ToolsFigma, FigJam

The brief

01

The problem

  • Government reports and surveys are static, outdated and hard to interpret.
  • Residents don't know which neighbourhoods are financially viable.
  • How can rent ranges and income distributions be overlaid to show where mismatches occur?
02

Design goals

  • Show rent ranges across neighbourhoods in an intuitive map view.
  • Overlay income data to identify affordability mismatches.
  • Filters for household size, income bracket and housing type.
  • Colour-coded visualisations for quick comparison.
  • An accessible, multilingual map for broad usability.
03

Impact

  • Citizens: a clearer view of which neighbourhoods they can realistically afford.
  • Policy makers: evidence-based insights to design subsidies, affordable housing projects and rent control measures.
  • Researchers: open data visualisations that support studies on inequality and urban growth.

Target users

Citizens

Individuals or families searching for affordable housing options.

Policy makers and city planners

Authorities analysing affordability to guide housing strategies and subsidies.

Researchers and NGOs

Organisations studying housing inequality and urban poverty.

Research and personas

I reviewed global housing affordability dashboards, such as Housing Affordability Index tools in U.S. cities, and compared them with Addis Ababa's housing reports. I found a gap in visual, neighbourhood-level accessibility for ordinary residents.

Iconography

UX principles

Usability principles behind the screens, and where they show up.

01

Immediate feedback

Sliders show their value as they move, so income, expenses and rent ratio can be adjusted by feel.

02

Progressive disclosure

The path runs from map to calculator to a recommended rent range, then to the neighborhoods that match.

03

Clarity

Plain labels and dollar ranges replace the tables found in static housing reports.

04

Color-coded comparison

Rent levels are color-coded on the map, so mismatches show up without reading figures.

Reflection and what's next

Future improvements

  • Real-time rent data integration from online listings.
  • Predictive modelling of housing costs under different growth scenarios.
  • Community-driven reporting of rental prices for richer datasets.
